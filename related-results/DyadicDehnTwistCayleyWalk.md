# A fixed-generator Cayley walk on the dyadic Dehn-twist shadow

## Status

This note strengthens the algorithmic part of the
[public Roe-inspired follow-on](../ROE_2ADIC.md). Unlike the coordinate-refresh
chain, the walk below is a genuine state-independent random walk on a fixed
generating set. It is generated by two finite Dehn twists and three unit
scalings.

The theorem is an elementary spectral estimate for one explicit finite affine
group. It is a deduction recorded here, not a claim of priority. General
representation-theoretic treatments of affine random walks include the work
of Ayyer--Steinberg; the purpose of this calculation is to keep the
Roe--Turturean Dehn-twist coordinate visible.

## 1. The walk

For $m\geq4$, put

$$
R_m=\mathbb Z/2^m\mathbb Z,
\qquad
G_m=R_m\rtimes R_m^\times,
$$

with multiplication

$$
M(u,b)M(v,c)=M(uv,b+uc).
$$

Let $T=M(1,1)$, $D=M(5,0)$, and $J=M(-1,0)$. The elements $T$ and
$T^{-1}$ are the finite images of the dyadic Dehn twists
$\mathcal T_1$ and $\mathcal T_{-1}$.

Define the Markov operator

$$
\begin{aligned}
(P_mf)(u,b)
={}&\frac12f(u,b)\\
&+\frac1{10}\bigl(
f(u,b+u)+f(u,b-u)
+f(5u,b)+f(5^{-1}u,b)+f(-u,b)
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Equivalently, this is the lazy right Cayley walk with step measure supported
on

$$
\{1,T,T^{-1},D,D^{-1},J\}.
$$

The measure is symmetric. Since $T$ generates all translations and
$-1,5$ generate $R_m^\times$, the walk is irreducible. It is reversible for
the uniform distribution.

## 2. Spectral-gap theorem

### Theorem

Let $\gamma_m=1-\lambda_2(P_m)$ be the spectral gap. Then

$$
\boxed{
\frac15\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^m}\right)
\leq
\gamma_m
\leq
\frac15\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^{m-2}}\right).
}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\gamma_m=\Theta(4^{-m})
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
t_{\mathrm{rel}}(P_m)=\Theta(4^m).
$$

More explicitly,

$$
\frac5{1-\cos(2\pi/2^{m-2})}
\leq
t_{\mathrm{rel}}(P_m)
\leq
\frac5{1-\cos(2\pi/2^m)}.
$$

If $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)$ denotes worst-case total-variation mixing
time, then the standard lazy reversible bound gives

$$
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)
\leq
\frac5{1-\cos(2\pi/2^m)}
\left((2m-1)\log2+\log\frac1\varepsilon\right).
$$

In particular,

$$
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)
=O\!\left(4^m\left(m+\log\frac1\varepsilon\right)\right).
$$

No cutoff assertion is made for this fixed-generator walk.

## 3. Proof

Write $\mathcal E_m(f,f)=\langle f,(I-P_m)f\rangle_\pi$. Split the
Dirichlet form into its translation and unit parts.

For fixed $u$, multiplication by the odd unit $u$ permutes $R_m$. Hence the
translation walk

$$
b\longmapsto b\pm u
$$

is the ordinary nearest-neighbor walk on the cycle of length $2^m$. Its gap
is

$$
\delta_b=1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^m}.
$$

The two translation moves have total probability $1/5$, so

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{tr}}(f,f)
\geq
\frac15\delta_b\,
\mathbb E_u\operatorname{Var}_b f(u,b).
\tag{1}
$$

For fixed $b$, the normalized unit walk chooses one of $5,5^{-1},-1$.
Using

$$
R_m^\times
\cong
C_{2^{m-2}}\times C_2,
\qquad
u=5^a(-1)^\sigma,
$$

its characters are indexed by $(k,s)$ and have eigenvalues

$$
\frac{
\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}+(-1)^s
}{3}.
$$

Here $\zeta$ is a primitive $2^{m-2}$-th root of unity. Thus this is
literally a cyclotomic trace calculation: $\zeta$ is a root of

$$
\Phi_{2^{m-2}}(X)=X^{2^{m-3}}+1.
$$

Under the complex embedding the eigenvalue is

$$
\frac{
2\cos(2\pi k/2^{m-2})+(-1)^s
}{3}.
$$

For $m\geq4$, its gap is therefore

$$
\delta_u
=
\frac23\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^{m-2}}\right).
$$

The three unit moves have total probability $3/10$. Thus

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{unit}}(f,f)
\geq
\frac15\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^{m-2}}\right)
\mathbb E_b\operatorname{Var}_u f(u,b).
\tag{2}
$$

Conditional expectation contracts variance, so

$$
\operatorname{Var}_u(\mathbb E_bf)
\leq
\mathbb E_b\operatorname{Var}_u f.
$$

Together with

$$
\operatorname{Var}f
=
\mathbb E_u\operatorname{Var}_b f
+\operatorname{Var}_u(\mathbb E_bf),
$$

equations (1)--(2) imply

$$
\mathcal E_m(f,f)
\geq
\frac15\min\left\{
1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^m},
1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^{m-2}}
\right\}\operatorname{Var}_\pi f.
$$

The first term is the minimum, proving the lower bound.

For the upper bound, take a nontrivial unit character

$$
f(5^a(-1)^\sigma,b)
=
\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ia}{2^{m-2}}\right),
$$

constant in $b$ and $\sigma$. The translation steps and $J$ fix it, while
$D,D^{-1}$ multiply it by the two conjugate roots of unity. Hence its
eigenvalue is

$$
1-\frac15\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{2^{m-2}}\right).
$$

This proves the upper bound. The relaxation and mixing estimates follow from
$t_{\mathrm{rel}}=1/\gamma_m$, $|G_m|=2^{2m-1}$, and the standard finite
reversible-chain inequality

$$
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)
\leq
t_{\mathrm{rel}}
\log\frac1{\varepsilon\pi_{\min}}.
\qquad\square
$$

## 4. Interpretation

The exact lift-bit sampler solves uniform generation without a walk. The
present theorem answers a different question: what happens if one is allowed
only a fixed collection of local group operations?

The answer separates the two mechanisms cleanly:

- the unit coordinate is moved by fixed scalings $5^{\pm1}$ and $-1$;
- the translation coordinate is moved by the fixed twists
  $\mathcal T_{\pm1}$;
- diffusion around the translation cycle forces relaxation on the order of
  $4^m$.

Thus a minimal fixed generator set pays a quadratic cost in the modulus,
whereas direct access to lift coordinates gives exact uniform sampling in
$2m-1$ bits. This is an algorithmic distinction inside the finite affine
shadow. It is not a statement about mixing in the full outer automorphism
group.
