# Group-ring Gauss congruences and the nonsofic torsion boundary

**Status date:** August 5, 2026

**Status:** complete elementary theorem and exact obstruction. The existence
and structure of the explicit nonsofic group are accepted from OpenAI's
Lean-certified result; the group-ring congruence theorem is proved here. No
claim of literature priority is made.

## 1. Coefficient-return sequences

Let $G$ be a group and let

```math
x=\sum_{g\in S}c_g g\in\mathbb Z[G]
```

have finite support. Define

```math
a_N=[1]x^N,
\tag{1}
```

the coefficient of the identity in the $N$-th group-ring power.

### Theorem 1 (torsion-free-at-$p$ Gauss tower)

Fix a prime $p$. If the subgroup $H=\langle S\rangle$ contains no element of
order $p$, then for all positive integers $n,r$,

```math
\boxed{
a_{np^r}\equiv a_{np^{r-1}}\pmod {p^r}.
}
\tag{2}
```

The conclusion is valid for arbitrary integer coefficients $c_g$, including
at $p=2$.

### Theorem 2 (the obstruction is exact)

For a fixed group $G$ and prime $p$, (2) holds for every finitely supported
$x\in\mathbb Z[G]$ if and only if $G$ has no element of order $p$.

Indeed, Theorem 1 proves one direction. For the converse, if $t\in G$ has
order $p$, take $x=t$. Then

```math
a_1=0,
\qquad a_p=1,
```

so even the first congruence fails.

## 2. Proof of Theorem 1

Replace each coefficient $c_g$ by $|c_g|$ colored copies of the letter $g$,
each copy carrying sign $\operatorname{sgn}(c_g)$. Then $a_N$ is the signed
number of length-$N$ colored words whose ordered product in $H$ is the
identity.

Cyclic rotation preserves this condition: if $g_1\cdots g_N=1$, then

```math
g_2\cdots g_Ng_1=g_1^{-1}(g_1\cdots g_N)g_1=1.
```

Partition the words by their minimal cyclic period $d\mid N$. A word of
minimal period $d$ is obtained by repeating a primitive block $N/d$ times.
If $h$ is the product of the group labels in that block, validity at length
$N$ says

```math
h^{N/d}=1.
\tag{3}
```

Put $N=np^r$. If $d\mid N/p$, then (3) and the absence of order-$p$ elements
imply $h^{N/(pd)}=1$: the finite cyclic group generated by $h$ has order
prime to $p$. Thus the same primitive block contributes at both adjacent
levels. Conversely, every valid block at level $N/p$ remains valid at level
$N$.

Each primitive cyclic orbit has exactly $d$ rotations. For odd $p$, its sign
is the same at both levels, because the two repetition counts differ by the
odd factor $p$. These contributions cancel in $a_N-a_{N/p}$.

At $p=2$, the signs can differ only when the primitive block has negative
sign and $N/(2d)$ is odd. In that case

```math
v_2(d)=v_2(N)-1
```

and the signed difference contributed by the orbit is $\pm2d$, divisible by
$2^{v_2(N)}$ and hence by $2^r$.

The remaining primitive periods satisfy $d\nmid N/p$. Therefore

```math
v_p(d)=v_p(N)\ge r.
```

Every corresponding orbit contribution is a multiple of its orbit size $d$
and hence of $p^r$. Summing all orbits proves (2). $\square$

## 3. Consequence for the explicit nonsofic group

OpenAI's accepted construction uses

```math
G=\operatorname{EL}_9(L_{\mathbb F_2}(1,2))
```

and contains a subgroup $J$ isomorphic to Thompson's group $V$. The standard
prefix-permutation realization of $V$ contains a cycle of every prime order:
choose a finite full binary prefix code with $p$ leaves and cyclically permute
those leaves.

Hence for every prime $p$ there is $t_p\in G$ of order $p$. With
$x_p=t_p$, the sequence

```math
a_N^{(p)}=[1]x_p^N
```

satisfies

```math
a_N^{(p)}=
\begin{cases}
1,&p\mid N,\\
0,&p\nmid N,
\end{cases}
```

and therefore fails $a_p\equiv a_1\pmod p$.

This gives a precise answer to the proposed bridge:

> Nonsoficity neither creates nor destroys group-ring Gauss congruences.
> Prime-order torsion is the exact obstruction to the universal
> coefficient-return theorem.

The positive theorem still applies to any finitely supported $x$ whose
support generates a subgroup without $p$-torsion, even when the ambient group
is nonsofic.

## 4. Relation to supercongruences

The exponent in (2) is the ordinary Gauss depth $r$, not the quadratic or
cubic depth occurring in the strongest OEIS problems. Stronger exponents
require additional cancellation, such as multinomial carries, reciprocal-unit
blocks, or Frobenius-balanced products.

The theorem nevertheless improves the program in two ways:

1. it produces a broad noncommutative source of exact Gauss towers; and
2. it gives an immediate torsion screen before any attempt to prove a stronger
   group-ring supercongruence.
