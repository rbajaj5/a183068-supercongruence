# Exact dyadic orientation lifts

## Status and source boundary

This note proves an elementary $2$-adic lemma that makes the lifting
phenomenon in Roe and Turturean's Remark C.7 completely explicit.

The source is David Roe and David Turturean,
[*A Presentation of the Absolute Galois Group of $\mathbb Q_2$*][RT],
especially Appendix C.5 and Remark C.7. Their paper supplies the motivating
orientation equation. The theorem below is a quantitative sharpening of that
remark, not a correction of their main classification theorem. No claim of
literature novelty is made here.

[RT]: https://roed314.github.io/gq2/paper.pdf
[Gropper]: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04309

## The orientation equation

Let $X\in\mathbb Z_2$ be the Hensel lift determined by

$$
X^3+2X^2+1=0,\qquad X\equiv5\pmod 8,
$$

and put

$$
\eta=-\frac13.
$$

Both $X$ and $\eta$ lie in $1+4\mathbb Z_2$. At precision $2^k$, the
orientation condition is

$$
X^e\equiv\eta\pmod {2^k}.
$$

### Theorem (exact orientation lift)

There is a unique $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_2$ such that

$$
X^\alpha=\eta.
$$

For every $k\ge3$,

$$
X^e\equiv\eta\pmod {2^k}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e\equiv\alpha\pmod {2^{k-2}}.
$$

If $e_k$ denotes the representative in
$\{0,\ldots,2^{k-2}-1\}$, then

$$
e_{k+1}\in\{e_k,\ e_k+2^{k-2}\}.
$$

Thus each new level either preserves the current representative or appends
one new high binary digit. The modulus of the exponent class doubles at every
level.

Finally, $\alpha$ is not an ordinary integer. Consequently the compatible
finite-level solutions do define a $2$-adic exponent, but they cannot all
come from one fixed finite exponent.

## Proof

The defining congruence gives

$$
v_2(X-1)=2,\qquad v_2(X+1)=1.
$$

For $j\ge1$, the $2$-adic lifting-the-exponent formula therefore gives

$$
v_2\!\left(X^{2^j}-1\right)
=v_2(X-1)+v_2(X+1)+j-1
=j+2.
$$

The same conclusion at $j=0$ is just $v_2(X-1)=2$. Hence the order of
$X$ modulo $2^k$, inside $1+4\mathbb Z/2^k\mathbb Z$, is exactly
$2^{k-2}$. That group also has $2^{k-2}$ elements, so $X$ generates it.
There is therefore one and only one exponent class modulo $2^{k-2}$
mapping to $\eta$.

Reduction from level $k+1$ to level $k$ makes these classes compatible.
Their inverse limit is a unique $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_2$. The two lifts of a
class modulo $2^{k-2}$ are exactly

$$
e_k\quad\text{and}\quad e_k+2^{k-2},
$$

which proves the binary lifting rule. The usual $2$-adic exponentiation on
$1+4\mathbb Z_2$, or equivalently continuity of the finite-level
isomorphisms, gives $X^\alpha=\eta$.

It remains to exclude $\alpha\in\mathbb Z$. Let $K=\mathbb Q(X)$. Since
$X$ has minimal polynomial $t^3+2t^2+1$,

$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(X)=-1.
$$

If $X^n=-1/3$ for an integer $n$, taking norms would give

$$
(-1)^n=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(-1/3)=-\frac1{27},
$$

which is impossible. Thus no fixed finite exponent solves every congruence.
$\square$

## The Dehn-twist layer

The same source contains a second exact structure that belongs in the lifting
stack. Write

$$
D_0=
\left\langle A,S,Y\ \middle|\ A^2S^4[S,Y]=1\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pro}\text{-}2}.
$$

Roe--Turturean equation (3.9) rewrites this as the cyclic HNN extension

$$
D_0=
\left\langle A,S,Y\ \middle|\
Y^{-1}SY=S^{-3}A^{-2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pro}\text{-}2}.
$$

This is precisely the graph-of-groups setting in which a stable-letter shear
is a Dehn twist. Gropper develops these automorphisms for Demushkin groups as
“arithmetic Dehn twists” and relates them to ordinary surface twists
([*Surfaces and p-adic fields I: Dehn twists*][Gropper]). The following
dyadic instance is completely explicit. Gropper's main classification
theorems are stated for odd $p$; the $p=2$ result below is instead checked
directly from Roe--Turturean's presentation.

### Theorem (exact pro-$2$ Dehn-twist tower)

For $b\in\mathbb Z_2$, define

$$
\mathcal T_b(A)=A,\qquad
\mathcal T_b(S)=S,\qquad
\mathcal T_b(Y)=S^bY.
$$

Then:

1. $\mathcal T_b$ is a continuous automorphism of $D_0$;
2. $\mathcal T_b\mathcal T_c=\mathcal T_{b+c}$;
3. $b\mapsto[\mathcal T_b]$ is a continuous injection
   $$
   \mathbb Z_2\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Out}(D_0);
   $$
4. every $\mathcal T_b$ preserves the canonical orientation; and
5. on the free part of the abelianization, with ordered basis
   $(\bar S,\bar Y)$, it acts by
   $$
   \begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
   $$
   Consequently
   $$
   \mathcal T_b\equiv1\pmod {2^m}
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   b\in2^m\mathbb Z_2
   $$
   on the abelianization.

#### Proof

The defining HNN relation is preserved because

$$
(S^bY)^{-1}S(S^bY)=Y^{-1}SY.
$$

The inverse is $\mathcal T_{-b}$, and direct substitution gives the additive
composition law. Continuity follows from continuity of $b\mapsto S^b$ in
the procyclic subgroup generated by $S$.

On the abelianization,

$$
\bar S\longmapsto\bar S,\qquad
\bar Y\longmapsto\bar Y+b\bar S.
$$

Since $\bar S$ generates a free $\mathbb Z_2$-summand, this shear is
nontrivial for every $b\ne0$. Inner automorphisms act trivially on the
abelianization, so no nonzero $\mathcal T_b$ is inner. This proves the
injection into the outer automorphism group and the exact congruence-depth
statement.

Finally, the canonical orientation has

$$
\chi(A)=-1,\qquad \chi(S)=1,\qquad \chi(Y)=\eta.
$$

Thus multiplying $Y$ by $S^b$ does not change its orientation value.
$\square$

### Relation with the automorphisms in the source

Roe--Turturean Proposition 3.9 uses

$$
\Theta_b(A)=A^{S^b},\qquad
\Theta_b(S)=S,\qquad
\Theta_b(Y)=YS^b.
$$

With their right-conjugation convention,
$\Theta_b=\mathrm{conj}_{S^b}\circ\mathcal T_b$: conjugation by $S^b$
is applied after the twist. Hence the two families determine the same element
of $\mathrm{Out}(D_0)$. The “shear” used in their marking argument is
therefore literally the outer class of a Dehn twist.

## The affine symmetry behind Proposition 3.9

The twist subgroup is the translation half of a larger exact affine
structure. Roe--Turturean decompose the abelianization as

$$
B_{\mathrm{ab}}
=C_2t\oplus\mathbb Z_2\bar S\oplus\mathbb Z_2\bar Y
$$

and prove that every orientation-preserving automorphism has a unique pair
$(u,b)\in\mathbb Z_2^\times\times\mathbb Z_2$ with

$$
t\longmapsto t,\qquad
\bar S\longmapsto u\bar S,\qquad
\bar Y\longmapsto\bar Y+b\bar S.
$$

### Theorem (affine shadow and exact commutator depth)

There is a canonical isomorphism

$$
\mathrm{Aut}(B_{\mathrm{ab}},\chi)
\cong
\mathbb Z_2\rtimes\mathbb Z_2^\times,
$$

where the corresponding matrices on $(\bar S,\bar Y)$ are

$$
M(u,b)=
\begin{pmatrix}
u&b\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$

and

$$
M(u,b)M(v,c)=M(uv,b+uc).
$$

Moreover:

1. the natural map
   $$
   \mathrm{Out}(D_0)\longrightarrow
   \mathrm{Aut}(B_{\mathrm{ab}},\chi)
   $$
   is surjective;
2. the translation subgroup $M(1,b)$ has the explicit Dehn-twist section
   $b\mapsto[\mathcal T_b]$;
3. diagonal scaling conjugates translation by
   $$
   M(u,0)M(1,b)M(u,0)^{-1}=M(1,ub);
   $$
4. with the source convention
   $[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh$,
   $$
   [M(u,0),M(1,b)]
   =M\!\left(1,(1-u^{-1})b\right);
   $$
   hence, whenever $(u-1)b\ne0$,
   $$
   v_2\!\left((1-u^{-1})b\right)
   =v_2(u-1)+v_2(b);
   $$
5. the closed commutator subgroup of the affine shadow is exactly the
   subgroup of translations by $2\mathbb Z_2$, and its lower central series is
   $$
   \gamma_n=2^{n-1}\mathbb Z_2
   \qquad(n\ge2)
   $$
   inside the translations.

#### Proof

The matrix formula gives the multiplication law and the semidirect product.
Surjectivity is Roe--Turturean Proposition 3.9; inner automorphisms act
trivially on the abelianization, so their map factors through
$\mathrm{Out}(D_0)$. The preceding Dehn-twist theorem supplies the
translation section.

The conjugation and commutator formulas follow by multiplying the displayed
matrices. Since $u$ is a $2$-adic unit,

$$
1-u^{-1}=u^{-1}(u-1),
$$

which proves the valuation identity.

Every $2$-adic unit is odd, so $u-1\in2\mathbb Z_2$. All commutators
therefore translate by an element of $2\mathbb Z_2$. Conversely $u=3$
gives

$$
1-u^{-1}=\frac23,
$$

whose valuation is exactly $1$; varying $b$ produces all of
$2\mathbb Z_2$. Repeating the same argument on
$2^r\mathbb Z_2$ proves the lower-central-series formula.
$\square$

### Finite-level consequence

Modulo $2^m$, the affine shadow has exactly

$$
2^m\varphi(2^m)=2^{2m-1}
$$

elements for $m\ge1$. Every reduction from level $m+1$ to level $m$
has a four-element kernel: two choices for the new unit digit and two choices
for the new translation digit. This is a literal two-coordinate lifting
tower rather than an analogy with one.

The theorem concerns the affine quotient of the outer automorphism group.
Proposition 3.9 supplies lifts of the unit-scaling matrices, but not a
canonical multiplicative section of the entire affine group. No such section
is asserted here.

### A nonzero linear obstruction proxy

The most immediate abelian coefficient calculation does not vanish. Let

$$
U=\mathbb Z_2^\times,
\qquad
T=\mathbb Z_2
$$

with the natural action $u\cdot b=ub$. For continuous cohomology of profinite
modules,

$$
\boxed{H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(U,T)\cong\mathbb Z/2.}
$$

Indeed,

$$
U\cong C_2\times\Gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma=1+4\mathbb Z_2=\overline{\langle5\rangle}.
$$

The completed procyclic resolution gives

$$
H^0(\Gamma,T)=\ker(5-1)=0,
\qquad
H^1(\Gamma,T)=T/4T\cong\mathbb Z/4,
$$

and no higher $\Gamma$-cohomology. The element $-1\in C_2$ acts by
negation on $\mathbb Z/4$. The Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence
therefore has only its $q=1$ row, and in total degree two gives

$$
H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(U,T)
\cong
H^1(C_2,\mathbb Z/4_{-})
\cong
\frac{\mathbb Z/4}{2(\mathbb Z/4)}
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
$$

This calculation rules out a purely formal vanishing argument for that
linear coefficient module. It does **not** decide whether the surjection

$$
\mathrm{Out}(D_0)\twoheadrightarrow
T\rtimes U
$$

splits. If $K$ is its kernel and $\widetilde u$ are chosen lifts of unit
scalings, the actual factor set

$$
c(u,v)=\widetilde u\,\widetilde v\,\widetilde{uv}^{-1}
$$

takes values in $K$, which need not be abelian or identified with $T$.
The displayed $\mathbb Z/2$ becomes an obstruction group for the original
problem only after constructing a compatible abelian pushout
$K\to T$ and identifying the pushed-forward factor set. No such
identification is claimed here.

### Equivariance of the defect tower

The filtration $\lambda_k$ in Appendix C.5 is characteristic, so every
$\mathcal T_b$ descends to the finite quotients $Q_k=D_0/\lambda_k$ and
their central layers $Z_k$. Applying $\mathcal T_b$ to every entry of a
marked triple preserves:

- generation;
- vanishing of the tested relator; and
- the orientation clause.

It therefore acts compatibly on every stage set $S_k$. Naturality of word
evaluation gives

$$
\delta_k(\mathcal T_bT)=\mathcal T_b\delta_k(T),
$$

The correction homomorphism $\bar d_k$ is assembled from group operations and
commutators with the entries of the marked triple itself. Applying an
automorphism to the triple therefore transports every bracket and operand in
its defining formula, which proves the same equivariance for $\bar d_k$ and
the resulting corrections. Thus the twist does not create a separate lifting
argument: it is a $\mathbb Z_2$-symmetry of the $D_0$ half of the existing
one. Appendix C.5 also runs a lifting construction in the reverse direction;
no corresponding twist family for $D_R$ is constructed here.

## What Remark C.7 does and does not show

The finite congruences are perfectly compatible and converge in the profinite
sense. The obstruction is not a failure of inverse-limit compactness.
Instead, the limiting $2$-adic exponent is outside the discrete set of
ordinary integer exponents.

Accordingly, the phrase that representatives “grow like” a power of $2$
should be read as growth of the available exponent modulus, not monotone
growth of the least nonnegative representative. A representative can remain
unchanged for several levels before acquiring another high binary digit.

This is a useful boundary example for lifting arguments: levelwise solvability
plus compatibility produces a profinite solution, but an additional
closedness or bounded-complexity argument is needed to recover a finite
discrete witness.

architectural: the same dyadic discipline appears when a congruence class
lifts through successively doubled moduli. Exact order, compatibility, and
